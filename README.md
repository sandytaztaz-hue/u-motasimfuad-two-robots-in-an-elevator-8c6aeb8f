Entrants submit a single line, under 200 characters, of awkward small talk that one robot says to another while they ride an elevator together. A judge model reads the lines two at a time against a brief published with the bounty and picks the funnier one, weighing whether it is really a robot's elevator line, whether machine logic shows through, whether the awkwardness lands, and whether it is funny on the first read. Three private blind rounds decide the ranking, so no score or place shows while entries are open, and the top places share the reward pool.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

The program writes one line to `/output/entry.txt` — awkward robot small talk in 134 characters, inside the 200-character gate. After 214 shared elevator rides, one robot admits it does not know the other's name — but it knows the floor, the weight, and the exact pitch of the sigh. Robot logic showing through the politeness, the awkwardness landing on the thing neither robot wants to name, turn at the end — and none of the field's tired weather-with-sensor-readings, silence-percentage or "I calculated 14,000 things to say" cracks. Field sampled 2026-11-25: 1,524 entries, weather and silence jokes dominate, so this line takes the 214-rides angle instead.

> We have shared this elevator 214 times. I do not know your name, but I know your floor, your weight, and the exact pitch of your sigh.
